Name your brand.

Six names, generated against the same nine heuristics we use ourselves — short, early in the alphabet, survives being heard once aloud, and actually means something. Then we check the domains, the handles and the trademark register — and say plainly which ones we could not check.

The heuristics

  • Fewer syllables the better.
  • First letter close to A — alphabetical-sort advantage.
  • Passes the radio test — heard once, a listener can spell it.
  • Represents what you do, at least metaphorically.
  • Unique in spelling — ownable in search results.
  • Meaning tied to its origin is a plus.
  • Available as a .com or another TLD.
  • Easy to type — no punctuation, numerals or accents.
  • Travels well — doesn't mean something bad elsewhere.

Availability is not clearance. Domain and handle checks are live lookups that can go stale within the hour, and the trademark check finds exact matches only — a name with no exact match can still infringe a similar mark in the same class. Confirm anything you intend to rely on. Nabu builds the rest of the brand.
